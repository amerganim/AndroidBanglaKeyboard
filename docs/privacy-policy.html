Privacy Policy — Amader Bangla Keyboard

Last updated: 2026-06-07

Amader Bangla Keyboard (“the app”) is an on-screen keyboard (input method) for Android. This policy explains what the app does and does not do with your data.

Summary

The app does not collect, transmit, or share any of your data. Everything stays on your device. The app has no internet permission, so it is technically incapable of sending your typing anywhere.

What stays on your device

To work as a keyboard the app processes the text you type only on your device, in memory. The following is stored only in the app’s private storage and never leaves your device:

  • words you have typed (your personal dictionary);
  • how often you pick suggestions (learning data);
  • next-word statistics learned from your typing;
  • your key-size and key-sound preferences.

Long-press a suggestion to forget it. Uninstalling the app deletes everything.

Sensitive fields

In password fields the app shows no suggestions and learns nothing. In fields that opt out of personalized learning, the app does not save or learn from what you type.

Permissions

  • Bind input method (BIND_INPUT_METHOD): required for any keyboard.
  • Microphone (RECORD_AUDIO): requested only when you tap the voice (🎤) key. If you never use voice typing, it is never requested.
  • No internet, contacts, location, or storage permissions.

Voice typing (speech-to-text)

Voice typing is optional. When you use the 🎤 key, the app uses your device’s system speech-recognition service to convert speech to text. The app itself does not record, store, or transmit audio, but the system speech service may process your audio under its own privacy policy and, depending on your device, may send it to its provider. If you prefer your voice never to leave the device, simply don’t use the 🎤 key — all other features work fully offline.

Data sharing

None. No analytics, no advertising, no third-party data-collecting SDKs.

Children’s privacy

The app collects no personal data from anyone, including children.
